# PHI-Bench: A Fail-Closed, Multi-Jurisdiction Evidence Pipeline for Structured Clinical-Study De-identification

**Status**: draft, 2026-07-07. Commit b454b9a plus this session's uncommitted working-tree changes (see `docs/paper/PAPER_EVIDENCE_PACK.md` for the exact file list and hashes).

## Abstract

Free-text clinical de-identification is a mature, actively-benchmarked field: transformer NER pipelines and LLM-in-the-loop systems now report F1 above 0.95 on the real, DUA-gated i2b2 2014 corpus. Multi-jurisdiction *structured* clinical-study data -- Case Report Form (CRF) exports with subject IDs, visit dates, and jurisdiction-specific national identifiers -- has no comparable evidence-first benchmark: no synthetic corpus with statutory authority citations spans India, the EU, Brazil, Australia, and Uganda alongside the US; no publicly reproducible pipeline demonstrates fail-closed structured de-identification (unparseable data quarantined rather than published raw) with utility-preserving transforms (interval-preserving date jitter, linkable pseudonymization) measured end to end; and no such system enforces, by construction, that an AI-assisted step never reads a row value. This paper presents PHI-Bench: (1) a deterministic, seed-reproducible synthetic corpus generator spanning 7 jurisdiction groups (886 records, 2,506 gold-annotated spans, seed 42) plus a new CRF-style tabular generator for India and USA clinical studies; (2) `phi_engine`, a fail-closed structured scrub pipeline (HMAC-SHA256 pseudonymization, SANT per-subject date jitter, rule-based drop/cap/generalize, and a header-only AI-assist layer with a deterministic fallback); and (3) an adapter-based benchmark harness comparing `phi_engine`'s own detection surface against Microsoft Presidio (stock and tuned) and spaCy on identical corpora under a pre-registered strict/legacy dual scoring protocol. We report, as measured and bounded to this evidence: 99.17% (India) and 99.58% (USA) redaction recall over 719 gold-annotated PHI cells per jurisdiction on a synthetic 60-subject CRF cohort, zero residual PHI findings, 100% cross-form pseudonym-token linkage, and a `phi_engine` detection surface that outperforms stock/tuned Presidio's strict-protocol F1 on every evaluated jurisdiction (0.51 vs 0.10 on India; 0.37 vs 0.32/0.34 on USA), attributable to dedicated non-US structured-identifier recognizers Presidio's predefined set lacks. We do not claim free-text NER competitiveness; that positioning is explicitly disclaimed in Section 6.

## 1. Introduction

Clinical-study data pipelines that ingest CRF exports (screening logs, demographics forms, lab result sheets) face a narrower but no less consequential de-identification problem than free-text clinical notes: every column is a known, named field -- `SUBJID`, `VISITDAT`, `AADHAAR_NUM` -- so the detection problem reduces to *rule application*, but the disclosure risk is identical (a jurisdiction-specific national identifier or an exact visit date is Safe-Harbor / DPDPA / GDPR-regulated PHI regardless of whether it arrives as a table cell or a narrative sentence). Three properties matter for IRB-grade evidence in this setting that free-text NER benchmarks do not measure: (a) whether the pipeline is *fail-closed* -- does an unparseable date get published raw, or quarantined? -- (b) whether utility-preserving transforms (date jitter, pseudonymization) actually preserve the invariants they claim to (inter-visit intervals, cross-form subject linkage), and (c) whether any AI-assisted step in the pipeline can read a value, or only a column name (the "GR-1" invariant this system enforces by construction).

This paper's contribution is not a new de-identification algorithm. It is an **evidence-first pipeline**: a deterministic corpus generator with statutory citations per span, a fail-closed scrub engine measured against a purpose-built gold ledger (not merely "the tool ran without crashing"), and a benchmark harness that runs the system's own detection surface against open-source baselines on identical inputs under one scoring protocol. Every number in this paper traces to a committed artifact file and sha256 hash (`docs/paper/PAPER_EVIDENCE_PACK.md`).

## 2. Related Work

See `docs/SOTA_COMPARISON.md` for the full table (14 systems, every metric verified against its primary source on 2026-07-07) and the three-axis positioning argument reproduced in Section 6 below. In summary: free-text clinical de-identification is dominated by transformer NER (DeIDClinic/ClinicalBERT F1 0.9732 on i2b2 2014, arXiv 2410.01648; Bi-LSTM-CRF 95.50% strict micro-F1) and, since 2025, LLM-based systems (Microsoft Azure's de-identification service F1 0.939, GPT-4-0125 ten-shot F1 0.898, both on a 3,650-record UK multi-specialty corpus, PMC12719064; John Snow Labs Healthcare NLP v6.3.0 96% F1 vs GPT-4o's 79% on the same 2025 benchmark). General-purpose rule/NER hybrids (Microsoft Presidio, Philter) sit lower on real clinical text when independently re-evaluated outside their home benchmarks (Presidio P 0.51/R 0.74/F1 0.60, Philter P 0.35/R 0.79/F1 0.49, both on 200 neurosurgical documents, PMC12477974) -- a precision/recall tradeoff, not a strict ordering. None of these systems publish a structured-tabular, multi-jurisdiction, fail-closed evaluation; i2b2 2014 itself is US-only and DUA-gated.

### 3.1 New: clinical-study tabular generator

`generators/study_tabular.py` adds `IndiaStudyTabularGenerator` and `USStudyTabularGenerator`, emitting header-keyed flat-dict rows -- the exact shape `phi_engine.security.phi_scrub` reads -- across three CRF forms (`1A_Screening.jsonl`, `2_Demographics.jsonl`, `3_Labs.jsonl`) per synthetic subject cohort. Column contract binds to the packaged scrub-rule defaults (`phi_engine/config/_defaults/phi_scrub.yaml`), verified programmatically (not assumed) against `PHIScrubConfig`'s own matching methods before any row was generated. Three deliberate fail-closed edge cases are planted at fixed subject indices in every cohort of size >= 5, so the fail-closed path is exercised, not merely present in the code: an age-92 subject (Safe-Harbor cap boundary), two subjects with a genuinely unparseable visit date, and two subjects with a missing subject ID on one form only (orphan-row quarantine). A companion gold ledger (`gold_ledger.jsonl`) records, per cell, the expected scrub action and the raw pre-scrub value -- written only to the run's evidence output directory, never into the staging tree the scrub engine itself processes.

## 4. System

`phi_engine`'s scrub layer (`phi_engine/security/phi_scrub.py`) applies a fixed priority chain per column: force-drop, keep-allowlist, posture-dependent birthdate handling, drop, age-cap (Safe Harbor >89), generalize, band, small-cell suppression, SANT per-subject date jitter, then HMAC-SHA256 domain-separated pseudonymization. Every rung is fail-closed by construction: an unparseable date is blanked (or the row quarantined, per configured policy) rather than published; an unmapped generalize/band value quarantines its row; a row with no resolvable subject ID moves to a quarantine directory that is never on the publish path.

**AI proposes, determinism disposes.** `phi_engine.security.llm_detector.classify_headers` reads column *headers only* -- never a row value -- and, when a configured LLM is unreachable (`llm.provider: none`, the shipped default; no cloud LLM was configured or reached in any run reported here), falls back to a deterministic `presidio_gate` + `phi_gate` pattern classifier over header text. This is the GR-1 invariant: no measured run in this paper involved an LLM reading patient data, verified by direct inspection of `classify_headers`'s call graph and by the `classifier_path: "fallback"` field recorded in every `phi_system_result.json`.

**Detection benchmark harness.** `benchmarks/*_adapter.py` share one metrics module (`benchmarks/metrics.py`: span-overlap and exact-match scoring, per-entity-type/HIPAA-category/detection-regime breakdowns, macro-F1, structural-gap accounting) and one protocol (`benchmarks/protocol.py`: primary profile `strict_all_span`, secondary `legacy_overlap_coverable`, version `2026-07-06.strict-v1`). `benchmarks/phi_engine_adapter.py` (new this session) exposes `phi_engine`'s own detection surface as an evaluated tool under this identical harness -- the gap the evidence plan set out to close (Ground truth: "No adapter exists for phi_engine's own detection surface").

## 5. Evaluation and Results

### 5.1 System run (Phase 3): fail-closed scrub, measured against a gold ledger

Two demo studies, 60 synthetic subjects each, seed 42; India run first (locked user decision), then USA; a third India run at seed 1337 as an anti-tuning replication.

\input{../paper_assets/table_system.tex}

Every fail-closed edge case matched its planted count exactly (quarantine 2/2, blank 2/2, cap 1/1) in every run. Cross-form pseudonym-token linkage was 60/60 subjects and pseudonym regex compliance was 238/238 cells (100%) in every run. Per-subject date-jitter offsets were internally constant (60/60 subjects) and inter-visit intervals were exactly preserved (56/56 checkable subject-pairs) in every run. All measured "leaks" (6 in India, 3 in USA) are explained: they are date cells whose deterministic per-subject offset computed to exactly zero -- a disclosed, quantified 1-in-61 property of the additive-noise jitter scheme, not an unexplained detection failure. The held-out seed-1337 replication is byte-for-byte identical in every one of these measurements to the seed-42 India run, the anti-tuning signal this evidence pack is designed to produce.

### 5.2 Detection benchmark (Phase 4): `phi_engine` vs. open-source baselines, identical corpora

\input{../paper_assets/table_benchmark.tex}

`phi_engine`'s own detection surface has the highest strict-protocol F1 of every fully-evaluated tool on every one of the six jurisdictions tested, most dramatically on India (0.51 vs. 0.10 for both Presidio configurations) where stock Presidio's predefined recognizer set has no Aadhaar, PAN, or Indian-voter-ID equivalent. On the US corpus the margin narrows (0.37 vs. 0.32/0.34) because Presidio's predefined set is itself US/HIPAA-oriented; `phi_engine` also has the *highest* structural-gap rate on US (59.3% vs. Presidio's 24.7%), meaning it covers fewer HIPAA Safe-Harbor categories overall even as it detects the categories it does cover more precisely. Philter and pydeid could not be meaningfully exercised in this environment (both installed-but-nonfunctional findings independently confirmed via source inspection and web research, not assumed -- `docs/SOTA_COMPARISON.md` Section 1 and `benchmarks/results/comparison_table.md`); they are recorded `not_run` with a precise reason rather than a fabricated result.

## 6. Discussion: what this system does and does not claim

This system is not evaluated against, and does not claim to beat, free-text clinical NER SOTA (Section 2). The defensible axes, evidenced above and detailed in `docs/SOTA_COMPARISON.md`: (a) a fail-closed structured/tabular CRF pipeline with *measured* utility-preserving transforms, a category no surveyed system publishes evidence for; (b) GR-1 -- no LLM ever reads a row value, structurally different from RedactOR, GPT-4-ten-shot, and Llama-3-based anonymizers, all of which send document content to an LLM; (c) the first evidence-first multi-jurisdiction synthetic PHI benchmark with span-level gold and statutory citations, where i2b2 2014's US-only DUA-gated real-patient corpus has no non-US, freely-reproducible counterpart.

Circularity (the system evaluated on its own synthetic generator's output) is a known limitation, mitigated but not eliminated by: held-out-seed replication (Section 5.1), dual scoring profiles reported for every benchmark run, baselines run on byte-identical corpus files, and every artifact hashed and reproducible (`docs/paper/PAPER_EVIDENCE_PACK.md`). Non-US fairness is addressed by reporting the structural gap rate alongside every F1 number, rather than a bare aggregate score that would obscure it.

## 7. Limitations

Synthetic-only; no real patient or study data anywhere in this evidence pack. Single 60-subject cohort per seed (two seeds for India as an anti-tuning check, not a statistical power study; USA not independently seed-replicated in this pass). No clinician or counsel review has been performed (tracked as an open item, `.phi-build-status`). `header_classification_agreement` (21.4%, both jurisdictions) is a real, disclosed weakness of the deterministic (non-LLM) fallback classifier on short CRF column names, reported as measured rather than engineered upward, per this evidence pass's single-measured-run decision. Two open-source baselines (philter, pydeid) could not be exercised in this environment; the comparison proceeds honestly without them rather than fabricating rows.
