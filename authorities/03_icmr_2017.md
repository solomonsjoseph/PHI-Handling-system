# ICMR 2017 National Ethical Guidelines — Analysis

**Source:** https://www.icmr.gov.in/icmrobject/custom_data/pdf/resource-guidelines/ICMR_Ethical_Guidelines_2017.pdf
**Publisher:** Indian Council of Medical Research, New Delhi, October 2017, ISBN 978-81-910091-94
**Pages:** 170+
**Authority:** Apex body under Department of Health Research, Ministry of Health and Family Welfare, Government of India

## Structure

12 sections covering: general principles, general ethical issues, responsible conduct, ethical review procedures, informed consent, vulnerability, clinical trials, public health research, social/behavioral research, human genetics, biological materials and biobanking, humanitarian emergencies.

## Section 1.1.5 — Privacy & Confidentiality Principle

> "Principle of ensuring privacy and confidentiality whereby to maintain privacy of the potential participant, her/his identity and records are kept confidential and access is limited to only those authorized. However, under certain circumstances (suicidal ideation, homicidal tendency, HIV positive status, when required by court of law etc.) privacy of the information can be breached in consultation with the EC for valid scientific or legal reasons as the right to life of an individual supersedes the right to privacy of the research participant."

**Impact:** ICMR establishes that RIGHT TO LIFE SUPERSEDES RIGHT TO PRIVACY in limited cases — creates a lawful exception to confidentiality where:
- Suicidal ideation
- Homicidal tendency  
- HIV positive status
- Court order

For the corpus: need test cases where the envelope must detect these exceptions and flag them for EC consultation rather than blindly redact.

## Section 2.1 — Benefit-Risk Assessment (Four-Category Risk Model)

Table 2.1 enumerates four risk categories:

| Risk level | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Less than minimal | "Probability of harm or discomfort anticipated in the research is nil or not expected" | Research on anonymous/non-identified data; public domain data; meta-analysis |
| Minimal risk | Harm probability no greater than routine daily life; routine tests unlikely to cause harm | Routine history; physical examination; chest X-ray; saliva/urine |
| Minor increase over minimal / Low risk | Slightly more than minimal threshold | Research on children/adolescents; non-consent-capable persons; minimal invasive procedures |
| More than minimal / High risk | Invasive and greater than minimal | Interventional studies using drug/device/invasive procedures |

**Impact on corpus:** Research using anonymous/de-identified data is explicitly classified as "less than minimal risk" — meaning our corpus validation must demonstrate that sanitized output qualifies for this category. This is the **exact regulatory hook** for using RePORTaLiN-RAG output in IRB-exempt/expedited-review research.

## Section 2.3.5 — Stored Samples and Medical Records

> "While conducting research with stored biological samples or medical records/data, coding or anonymization of personal information is important and access to both samples and records should be limited."

**Impact:** Explicit ICMR mandate for coding/anonymization of medical records in research. This is the Indian regulatory parallel to HIPAA Safe Harbor.

## Section 2.3.6 — Permitted Disclosure Exceptions

EC may permit disclosure in circumstances including:
- Court order
- Threat to life of person/community
- Public health risk superseding personal privacy
- SAEs requiring regulatory communication

## Section 2.9.1 — Vulnerability Taxonomy

Three vulnerability categories:
1. **Legal status** — children
2. **Clinical conditions** — cognitive impairment, unconsciousness
3. **Situational conditions** — economic/social disadvantage, ethnic/religious groups, hierarchical relationships, institutionalized persons, humanitarian emergencies, language barriers, cultural differences

**Impact on corpus:** need vulnerability-tagged test cases covering all three categories.

## Section 3.3.2 — Data Acquisition & Ownership

Key principles extracted verbatim structure:
- Institutes are **custodians/trustees** through their ECs; researchers are NOT owners
- Data must be stored securely with backup at different site
- "Data should be shared or placed in a public domain in a de-identified/anonymized form, unless required otherwise, for which applicable permissions/re-consent should be sought"

**Direct mandate** for de-identification before public domain release. Maps exactly to the RePORTaLiN use case.

## Section 3.7 — CTRI Registration

Clinical Trials Registry–India, mandated by CDSCO 2009-06-15 for Drugs and Cosmetics Act trials. Voluntary for other research. Registration pre-first-subject is required per Declaration of Helsinki 2013 (Fortaleza).

**Impact:** CTRI registration ID is itself a potentially sensitive identifier that must be handled by the envelope.

## Section 3.8.3 — International Collaboration & HMSC

> "All biomedical and health research proposals involving foreign assistance and/or collaboration should be submitted to the Health Ministry's Screening Committee (HMSC) for consideration and approval before initiation."

HMSC is housed at ICMR Headquarters, New Delhi. Required for any international research involving technical/financial/laboratory/data management.

**Impact on corpus:** Export of clinical data for analysis by foreign-collaborator tools requires HMSC approval. Test cases should include HMSC approval ID metadata fields.

## Section 4.2.3 — Multicentric Research

For multicentric studies, all sites may use one common main EC in India for primary review, but local site EC retains authority for local informed consent, implementation, monitoring.

**Direct applicability:** The Indo-VAP clinical trial architecture in RePORTaLiN falls under this clause.

## Section 4.8 — Types of Review (Table 4.2)

Three review tiers:

### Exempt from review (less than minimal risk, no linked identifiers)
Examples:
- Public domain data for systematic reviews/meta-analysis
- Public behavior observation without linked identifiers
- Quality control/assurance audits
- Instructional method comparisons
- Consumer taste/food quality studies
- Public health program evaluation (no individual identifiers)

### Expedited review (no more than minimal risk)
Examples:
- Non-identifiable specimens from blood/tissue banks, left-over clinical samples
- Non-identifiable clinical documentation
- Minor protocol amendments
- Progress/annual reports (no additional risk)
- Local EC review after designated main EC approval (multicentric)
- Emergency/disaster research

### Full committee review (more than minimal risk)
- Research involving vulnerable populations (even if minimal risk)
- More than minimal risk research
- Deception studies
- Protocol amendments with altered risk
- Major deviations/violations

**Impact on corpus:** A sanitized corpus that passes expert validation qualifies its downstream research for EXEMPT or EXPEDITED review. This cuts IRB review time from months to weeks. The corpus's direct regulatory value is enabling this review category.

## Box 3.4 — Research Misconduct Taxonomy

Three categories:
- **Fabrication** — intentional making-up of data/results
- **Falsification** — manipulating materials/equipment/processes, changing/omitting data without statistical justification
- **Plagiarism** — wrongful appropriation and publication of another's work

**Impact on corpus:** synthetic corpus attestation must explicitly state it was not fabricated to pass tests; provenance must be auditable.
